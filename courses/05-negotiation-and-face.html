Negotiation and face

The contract is drafted and the numbers have been presented. Now the Shanghai side wants a different price, and the conversation that follows is where register, patience and one cultural idea meet. Tonight has three parts: what face means in a negotiation, as two researchers argued it in the Harvard Business Review, and why that is a tendency to watch for rather than a rule to apply; three softened refusals that are very common in business Chinese, and how to read them without pushing the other side into a yes they cannot give; and how to propose a concession with an if-clause, trade it for something back, and close on a next step with a date. You leave having written all six turns of a price negotiation, each one checked for register.

Part 1 · covers face in a negotiation

面子 (miànzi, face)

5 min live

面子 (miànzi, face) is the standing a person holds in the eyes of others. In a negotiation it is at stake on both sides of the table at once. John L. Graham and N. Mark Lam, writing in the Harvard Business Review in October 2003, put the risk in one line: "a broken promise or display of anger or aggression causes mutual loss of face". Mutual is the word to hold on to. The person who breaks the promise or loses their temper loses face as well as the person on the receiving end.

LiveFace, as Graham and Lam put it3 min▶

The source is John L. Graham and N. Mark Lam, "The Chinese Negotiation", Harvard Business Review, October 2003. The line quoted above is theirs: "a broken promise or display of anger or aggression causes mutual loss of face". They treat face as a kind of social capital that both negotiators hold and can spend or lose. Read that way, an angry outburst is not only rude: it costs the person who makes it, and it costs the person who has to sit through it in front of colleagues.

Two practical habits follow directly from their line. Promise only what you can deliver, including small things such as "I will send the revised terms today", because a small promise broken still counts. And when a number frustrates you, say so in a level voice and in words, not in tone: 目前的价格对我们来说有一定难度 (mùqián de jiàgé duì wǒmen lái shuō yǒu yídìng nándù, the current price is somewhat difficult for us) says the same thing as anger without the cost.

The other items on the scale, giving credit in public, disagreeing in private, leaving room for a later answer, are common professional practice. No source in the map behind these pages states them, and the figure labels them that way.

Self-studyA tendency, not a rule: the disagreement3 min▶

Any description of "how Chinese people negotiate" is a generalisation about a very large and varied group of people and companies. Writers on culture and negotiation disagree about how much such generalisations help, and that disagreement is worth knowing rather than settling here.

One view: cultural patterns such as face are a useful first map. They explain behaviour a newcomer would otherwise misread, such as a polite non-answer, and they prompt you to protect the other side's standing, which costs you nothing.

The other view: the person across the table is an individual first. Their company, industry, generation, training and personal style may matter more than national culture, and a negotiator who arrives with a list of "Chinese traits" can stereotype the very person they need to read, or miss that the other side is as direct as anyone in Singapore.

The working position for these pages: treat face as a tendency to watch for, not a prediction. Notice what the actual person does, and adjust. Manager Wang is a constructed character; the real Manager Wang you meet may run his meetings in a way no article describes.

Real world

The promise that was too small to matter. A constructed case: Lumen Logistics, 鲁门物流 (Lǔmén Wùliú, Lumen Logistics), Manager Wang and every scenario on this page are invented for the running case. In an earlier round, a Lumen manager closed a call with "I will send the volume forecast tomorrow" and sent it four days later. Nobody complained. But in the next meeting Manager Wang's team checked every figure twice, and the price conversation started from caution rather than trust. The fix was dull and effective: from then on, Lumen only named a date it had already cleared internally.

Part 2 · covers the indirect no

The indirect no

5 min live

A flat 不行 (bù xíng, no, that will not work) across a negotiating table is rare in business Chinese, and part of the reason is the scale in Part 1: a hard no can cost face on both sides. What you hear instead is a softened phrase. Three are very common: 再考虑考虑 (zài kǎolǜ kǎolǜ, let us think about it more), 我们研究一下 (wǒmen yánjiū yíxià, we will look into it) and 有一定难度 (yǒu yídìng nándù, it is somewhat difficult). This is common usage that any business speaker will recognise, not a finding from a published source, and how firm each one is depends on who says it and how.

Self-studyReading them without forcing a yes2 min▶

The mistake learners make is to hear "we will look into it" as an agreement in progress and then press: "so is that a yes?". That turns a softened no into a public one, which is exactly the face cost Part 1 described. The better move is one of the open follow-ups above, or a new proposal that changes the terms, which is Part 3. Either keeps the other side's standing intact and the conversation alive.

You can also use the phrases yourself. When a number does not work for Lumen, 有一定难度 (yǒu yídìng nándù, it is somewhat difficult) tells Manager Wang so without the flat 不行 (bù xíng, no, that will not work), and turn 1 of the build-along does exactly that.

A note on the pinyin: 一下 (yíxià, a little, briefly) and 一定 (yídìng, certainly, a certain) are written here with the tone you say, yí before a fourth tone. Standard pinyin (GB/T 16159-2012) writes the original tone, yī, and allows the changed tone to be written in teaching, which is what these pages do.

Part 3 · covers concessions and the next step

Concessions and the next step

4 min live

When the other side signals no, the answer is a new proposal, and the frame for it is 如果 ... 的话 (rúguǒ ... de huà, if ..., in that case): if your company could do this, we could do that. Every 让步 (ràngbù, concession) Lumen makes is attached to something it gets back. And the negotiation does not end with agreement; it ends with a next step, an owner and a date.

Live如果 ... 的话 (rúguǒ ... de huà, if ..., in that case): propose a trade2 min▶

如果 (rúguǒ, if) opens the condition and 的话 (de huà, in that case) closes it; the offer follows. In business writing the pattern carries both halves of a trade in one sentence:

如果贵公司能把单价降低百分之五的话 (rúguǒ guì gōngsī néng bǎ dānjià jiàngdī bǎi fēn zhī wǔ de huà, if your company could lower the unit price by five percent)，我司可以把合同期延长到两年 (wǒ sī kěyǐ bǎ hétongqī yáncháng dào liǎng nián, we could extend the contract term to two years)。

The condition comes first, so the other side hears what they would get before they hear what they would give. Either half can be shortened in speech; 的话 (de huà, in that case) is often dropped, and 如果 (rúguǒ, if) alone is still correct. This is ordinary grammar, at the level of any dictionary or textbook.

Self-studyWhy trade, and why end on a date2 min▶

Trade, do not give. A cut offered for nothing tells the other side the first price had room in it, and invites the next request. A cut offered for something, a longer term, a faster payment, a larger volume, keeps both sides' positions intact: each can report the deal upstairs as something they won. That is common negotiation practice, taught widely, not a finding from the source on this page.

End on a date. Here Graham and Lam's line returns. A next step with a date is a promise, and a broken promise costs face on both sides. So name only a date you have cleared: 修改后的条款我今天下班前发您 (xiūgǎi hòu de tiáokuǎn wǒ jīntiān xiàbān qián fā nín, I will send you the revised terms before the end of today) is a promise worth making only if it will be kept.

Real world

The two percent that bought nothing. A constructed case. Early in the talks, keen to be agreeable, a Lumen manager offered two percent off "as a gesture" and asked for nothing back. Manager Wang thanked him and, at the next meeting, asked for three more. The reset came from attaching every later move to a condition, 如果 ... 的话 (rúguǒ ... de huà, if ..., in that case), and the deal that was finally signed traded price against term and payment, never against nothing.

Build-along

Six turns of a price negotiation

★ 22 min · everyone writes

Manager Wang has come back on the unit price in the draft contract, and the two of you settle it over WeChat. Six turns: three are Lumen's, which you write, and three are his, which you read and answer. Each turn climbs one rung of the Part 3 ladder, and each is checked against the WeChat band with the formality checker from session 3. The numbers in the steps are what the checker computes on the Chinese printed here, without the pinyin and English.

Turn 1 · state the position, calmly. Address him by title, name the quote, and say the price does not work with the softened phrase from Part 2. Compare the pushy version a frustrated learner might type: 你们公司这个价太高了 (nǐmen gōngsī zhège jià tài gāo le, your company's price is too high)，赶紧降一点吧 (gǎnjǐn jiàng yìdiǎn ba, hurry up and bring it down a bit)，不然没法谈 (bùrán méi fǎ tán, or there is nothing to talk about)。 The checker puts that at index 34, WeChat fit 76, too casual; and it is the "display of anger or aggression" Graham and Lam warn about.

★ Turn 1 · you, for Lumen王经理您好 (Wáng jīnglǐ nín hǎo, hello, Manager Wang)，关于贵公司上周发来的报价 (guānyú guì gōngsī shàng zhōu fā lái de bàojià, about the quote your company sent last week)，我司内部已经讨论过了 (wǒ sī nèibù yǐjīng tǎolùn guò le, we have discussed it internally)。 目前的价格对我们来说有一定难度 (mùqián de jiàgé duì wǒmen lái shuō yǒu yídìng nándù, the current price is somewhat difficult for us)。 (In English: Hello, Manager Wang. We have discussed the quote your company sent last week internally. The current price is somewhat difficult for us.) Checker, WeChat to a partner's manager: index 59 · fit 100

Turn 2 · read his reply, and do not press. He does not say no. He explains, and then uses 我们研究一下 (wǒmen yánjiū yíxià, we will look into it). Asking "so is that a yes?" here would force a public no. A new proposal is the better answer.

★ Turn 2 · Manager Wang理解 (lǐjiě, understood)。 不过这个价格已经很接近我们的成本了 (búguò zhège jiàgé yǐjīng hěn jiējìn wǒmen de chéngběn le, but this price is already very close to our cost)。 我们研究一下 (wǒmen yánjiū yíxià, we will look into it)，再给您回复 (zài gěi nín huífù, and come back to you)。 (In English: Understood. But this price is already very close to our cost. We will look into it and come back to you.) Checker: index 51 · fit 100

Turn 3 · propose with 如果 ... 的话 (rúguǒ ... de huà, if ..., in that case). Thank him, then put the condition first and the offer second. Lumen asks for five percent off and offers a longer contract in return: a trade, not a gift.

★ Turn 3 · you, for Lumen感谢您 (gǎnxiè nín, thank you)。 如果贵公司能把单价降低百分之五的话 (rúguǒ guì gōngsī néng bǎ dānjià jiàngdī bǎi fēn zhī wǔ de huà, if your company could lower the unit price by five percent)，我司可以把合同期从一年延长到两年 (wǒ sī kěyǐ bǎ hétongqī cóng yì nián yáncháng dào liǎng nián, we could extend the contract term from one year to two)。 (In English: Thank you. If your company could lower the unit price by five percent, we could extend the contract term from one year to two.) Checker: index 62 · fit 100

Turn 4 · read his counter-trade. He accepts the shape of the deal and moves both numbers: a smaller cut, and something back for his side, faster payment. Note that he trades too. A counter like this is progress, not a refusal.

★ Turn 4 · Manager Wang两年的话可以考虑 (liǎng nián de huà kěyǐ kǎolǜ, two years is something we can consider)。 如果单价降低百分之三 (rúguǒ dānjià jiàngdī bǎi fēn zhī sān, if the unit price comes down by three percent)，贵司是否能把付款期缩短到三十天 (guì sī shìfǒu néng bǎ fùkuǎnqī suōduǎn dào sānshí tiān, could your company shorten the payment term to thirty days)？ (In English: Two years is something we can consider. If the unit price comes down by three percent, could your company shorten the payment term to thirty days?) Checker: index 57 · fit 100

Turn 5 · summarise the deal and make one promise. Say the whole deal back in one sentence, so nobody remembers it differently, and promise only what you have cleared: the revised terms, today.

★ Turn 5 · you, for Lumen可以 (kěyǐ, that works)。 那我们按单价降低百分之三、两年合同、三十天付款来定 (nà wǒmen àn dānjià jiàngdī bǎi fēn zhī sān, liǎng nián hétong, sānshí tiān fùkuǎn lái dìng, then let us settle on three percent off the unit price, a two-year contract and payment in thirty days)。 修改后的条款我今天下班前发您 (xiūgǎi hòu de tiáokuǎn wǒ jīntiān xiàbān qián fā nín, I will send you the revised terms before the end of today)。 (In English: That works. Then let us settle on three percent off the unit price, a two-year contract and payment in thirty days. I will send you the revised terms before the end of today.) Checker: index 51 · fit 100

Turn 6 · the next step and a date. The top rung of the ladder. His reply names who acts, what happens and when. If he had not given a date, your next message would ask for one.

★ Turn 6 · Manager Wang好的 (hǎo de, all right)，请您发来后我们内部确认 (qǐng nín fā lái hòu wǒmen nèibù quèrèn, once you send them we will confirm internally)。 下周二上午十点开会 (xià zhōu'èr shàngwǔ shí diǎn kāihuì, a meeting next Tuesday at 10 am)，双方签字确认 (shuāngfāng qiānzì quèrèn, and both sides sign to confirm)。 (In English: All right. Once you send them, we will confirm internally. We meet next Tuesday at 10 am, and both sides sign to confirm.) Checker: index 53 · fit 100

Check your own turns. Rewrite turns 1, 3 and 5 with Lumen's real numbers or your own, typing the Chinese only (the pinyin and English above are for you, not for the checker). Choose "WeChat to a partner's manager you have met" and press check. If a turn drops below 42, the checker lists the casual words that pulled it down.

Write down the trade and the date. The checker counts register; it cannot tell whether a concession was traded or whether a date was named. Check those two yourself.

★ Step 8 · what to write downMy turn 1: index __ · my turn 3: index __ · my turn 5: index __ What I gave: __ · what I got back: __ The next step, its owner and its date: __ The checker cannot judge grammar, meaning or strategy: I read each turn once as Manager Wang would.

Write the trade in English before the Chinese. "If you ..., we can ..." in one English line first. Learners who start in Chinese tend to write the offer and forget the condition, and the if-clause is the part that makes it a trade.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · In Graham and Lam, "The Chinese Negotiation" (Harvard Business Review, October 2003), what do a broken promise or a display of anger or aggression cause?

Their line is "a broken promise or display of anger or aggression causes mutual loss of face". It is their argument about a tendency, held as a tendency: individuals and companies vary.

2 · Manager Wang answers your offer with 我们研究一下 (wǒmen yánjiū yíxià, we will look into it). What is the best next move?

In common usage the phrase is a not-yet and often a soft no. Pressing turns it into a public no, which costs face on both sides; an open question or a new proposal keeps the talk moving. This reading is common usage, not a sourced finding.

3 · Why does Lumen attach every price cut to something it gets back?

Trading is common negotiation practice, and the build-along shows both sides doing it: term for price, then payment for price. The next step with a date still comes after the trade, never instead of it.
