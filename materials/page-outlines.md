## 01-register.html · Register: the same sentence, three ways
h1: "Register: the same sentence, <span class="accent">three ways</span>". 🟠 Advanced. Footer first: "← Course home".
- Part 1 "Spoken, written, formal": the same request as a colleague chat, a WeChat to a partner, and a formal email; the three levers: person (你 / 您 (nǐ / nín, you / you polite)), question form (吗 / 是否 (ma / shìfǒu, question particle / whether)), company (你们公司 / 贵公司 (nǐmen gōngsī / guì gōngsī, your company casual / polite)). Figure: one sentence on three rows, the changed words highlighted, a channel label on each row.
- Part 2 "书面语 (shūmiànyǔ, written language)": written Chinese uses different words from speech: 是否 (shìfǒu, whether), 尽快 (jǐnkuài, as soon as possible), 稍后 (shāohòu, later); drawn as a two-column swap table in a figure.
- Part 3 "Over-formal": classical letter words (敝司 (bì sī, our humble company, classical), 兹 (zī, hereby, classical)) belong to printed letters; preview of session 3's ceiling. Figure: a ladder of formality with a ceiling bar.
- Build-along: rewrite a casual message for the other two channels; check with `#register-check` (load register-live.js).
- Quiz: the three levers; what 书面语 is; why over-formal is a fault. Covered: dictionary-level usage (secondary); constructed examples. Next: session 2.

## 02-formal-emails-and-wechat.html · Formal emails and WeChat
h1: "Formal emails and <span class="accent">WeChat</span>". 🟠 Advanced.
- Part 1 "The shape of a formal email": 尊敬的 (zūnjìng de, dear, respected) + title, 您好 (nín hǎo, hello, polite), the purpose in the first line, 请查收 (qǐng cháshōu, please find attached), 如有疑问 (rú yǒu yíwèn, if you have any questions), closing. 此致 敬礼 (cǐzhì jìnglǐ, with respect) as a widely observed convention, explicitly NOT a national standard (the map's correction). Figure: an email skeleton with each block labelled.
- Part 2 "The shape of a business WeChat": addressed by title, one purpose, short lines, no classical closings, voice messages only when invited (state as common practice). Figure: the same content as an email and as a WeChat, side by side.
- Part 3 "Subject lines and attachments": subject lines that name the item and date; file names in Chinese and English. Figure: three subject lines, weak to strong.
- Build-along: write Lumen's contract email and the WeChat that announces it, each with English beneath; check both with `#register-check` against their channels.
- Quiz: the five blocks of a formal email; why 此致 敬礼 is called a convention; what not to put in a WeChat. Next: session 3, the checker.

## 04-presenting-numbers.html · Presenting numbers
h1: "Presenting <span class="accent">numbers</span>". 🟠 Advanced.
- Part 1 "同比 (tóngbǐ, year on year) and 环比 (huánbǐ, period on period)": definitions from the National Bureau of Statistics (primary; quote the definitions in English): 同比 compares with the same period of the previous year; 环比 with the immediately preceding period. Figure: a 24-month bar strip with one 同比 arrow (this March vs last March) and one 环比 arrow (this March vs this February).
- Part 2 "Growth, decline, share": 增长 (zēngzhǎng, grow), 下降 (xiàjiàng, fall), 百分之二十 (bǎi fēn zhī èrshí, twenty percent), 占 (zhàn, account for). Figure: a mini chart with each part's Chinese label and English.
- Part 3 "Saying a result with its comparison": a sentence pattern for a board update; never a growth figure without saying which comparison. Real world: a constructed board update where 同比 was heard as 环比 and the story reversed. Figure: the same number told two ways.
- Build-along: present Lumen's three quarterly numbers (constructed, say so) with the right comparison word.
- Quiz: 同比 vs 环比; how to say 20 percent; why a growth figure needs its comparison. Covered: NBS (primary). Next: session 5.

## 05-negotiation-and-face.html · Negotiation and face
h1: "Negotiation and <span class="accent">face</span>". 🟠 Advanced.
- Part 1 "面子 (miànzi, face)": Graham and Lam, "The Chinese Negotiation", HBR, October 2003: face as social capital; "a broken promise or display of anger or aggression causes mutual loss of face". Teach it as their argument, cite it, and note that generalisations about a culture are tendencies, not rules (teach the disagreement). Figure: a two-sided scale showing what gives and costs face in a meeting.
- Part 2 "Indirect no": 我们研究一下 (wǒmen yánjiū yíxià, we will look into it), 有一定难度 (yǒu yídìng nándù, it is somewhat difficult), 再考虑考虑 (zài kǎolǜ kǎolǜ, let us think about it more) as common softened refusals (state as common usage, not sourced); how to read them without forcing a yes. Figure: three phrases on a scale from maybe to no.
- Part 3 "Concessions and the next step": proposing with 如果…的话 (rúguǒ … de huà, if ...), trading rather than giving, closing with a clear next step and date. Figure: a concession ladder with the next step at the top.
- Build-along: a scripted price negotiation for Lumen in six turns, each with English; the register of each turn checked with `#register-check` (load register-live.js).
- Quiz: what face is in Graham and Lam; reading an indirect no; why a concession is traded. Covered: Graham and Lam (primary citation); indirect phrases (common usage); Lumen constructed. Next: session 6.

## 06-dining-and-capstone.html · Business dining, and the capstone
h1: "Business dining, and the <span class="accent">capstone</span>". 🟠 Advanced (level chip 🟠 Capstone).
- Part 1 "At the table": the host invites and usually pays; seating with the guest of honour facing the door is widely described (state as widely described practice, not universal); 敬酒 (jìngjiǔ, propose a toast), 干杯 (gānbēi, cheers, bottoms up), and declining alcohol politely 我不太能喝 (wǒ bú tài néng hē, I cannot drink much). Figure: a round table seen from above with host and guest seats marked.
- Part 2 "客气 (kèqi, standing on ceremony)": ritual refusal and insistence; 别客气 (bié kèqi, please do not stand on ceremony). Figure: an offer-refuse-insist exchange as three bubbles.
- Part 3 the capstone and the final `.scorecard` (copy the markup from learn-asking-right-questions-as-da-with-phoebe/courses/06-the-mock-meeting-and-the-brief.html), title "The negotiation ship gate", eight rows: opening by title; a numbers update with 同比 / 环比 (tóngbǐ / huánbǐ, year on year / period on period) used correctly; an indirect no read correctly; a traded concession; a clear next step and date; a formal follow-up email in the email band on the checker; a WeChat confirmation in the WeChat band; a toast or a polite decline.
- Build-along: the full negotiation and both follow-ups, each checked with `#register-check`.
